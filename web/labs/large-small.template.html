Large and small color differences

Two colors can be judged a small step apart by one kind of judgment and a larger one by another. People comparing near matches side by side, and people laying out even steps along a scale, do not agree on how far apart vivid colors are. This page sets WLab, shaped for the first, beside its large-scale form, LSWLab, shaped for the second, and shows where the two part.

WLab is shaped so that a straight-line distance in it, ΔEWLab, reads like ΔE00: the color differences of near matches. Large-scale WLab (LSWLab) keeps WLab's lightness and hue and changes only its chroma, so that a straight-line distance, ΔELSWLab, reads like ΔE*ab, the plain distance in CIELAB, whose even steps are closer to how large steps are judged. Both are built on Wpt, so both describe the material and carry across lights and observers; every other lab uses WLab. An observing condition (hereafter "condition") is an observer under a light; CIELAB values below are the chosen condition's, with its white.

1. Two kinds of "equal"

Color differences of near matches are smaller, for the same distance in CIELAB, between vivid colors than between dull ones: ΔE94 and ΔE00 divide a chroma difference by 1 + 0.045 C*. WLab builds that into its chroma, a logarithm of Wpt's chroma whose strength depends on hue, so that a straight line in it does what those formulas do with weights. Even steps along a scale, as in the Munsell system, follow chroma more nearly in proportion, as ΔE*ab counts it, with no weighting.

LSWLab keeps the part of WLab's chroma correction that depends on hue, undoes the average logarithm, and scales chroma by 2.2 so that its distances are the size of ΔE*ab's. Its form without the scale, the scale 1, is the original definition; the scale is fitted here over the corrected Munsell colors, CIE 1931 under C (2.25 from chroma, 2.16 from differences), and changes the size of LSWLab's chroma, not its shape.

2. The Munsell colors in WLab, LSWLab and CIELAB

The corrected Munsell colors of one Value: each Hue's chips joined from gray outward (a spoke), each Chroma's chips joined round the circle (a ring). Munsell's Chromas were laid out as even steps, judged by eye, and each ring as one color strength whatever the Hue. In WLab the rings crowd toward the outside; in LSWLab they keep their spacing.

Rings: the RMS of each ring's chroma about its mean, in percent of the mean; 0% is a ring at one chroma all the way round. Steps: how evenly chroma grows from chip to chip along each Hue, the RMS of the chroma gained per step of Munsell Chroma, in percent of its mean; 0% is even steps. Hue: the RMS of hue angle along each Hue (WLab and LSWLab share hue). Each is a mean over the rings or Hues; both percentages are relative, so the spaces' sizes do not enter.

3. Differences, from neighbors to far apart

Pairs of the corrected Munsell colors, measured four ways: neighbors, one Munsell step apart (2 in Chroma, one of the 40 Hues, or 1 in Value), and random pairs from across the whole set. STRESS says how far one set of differences is from being in proportion to another: 0 when they are, larger the more they disagree. The scale is the factor between them (1 when they are the same size).

Random pairs. Left and middle: each space's differences against the formula it is shaped to follow, with the line where they are equal. Below: ΔELSWLab against ΔEWLab, each pair colored by how vivid it is (its mean CIELAB chroma): dull pairs fade into the page and vivid ones stand out. The two agree for dull pairs and part as the colors get more vivid.
