Profiles: a display for any observer

An RGB display makes its colors by mixing three lights, red, green and blue. An ordinary display profile (ICC version 2 or 4) records what those lights give one standard observer. An iccMAX (version 5) profile can record the lights themselves, wavelength by wavelength, so that the display's colors can be worked out for any observer.

On this page you can:

  • describe a display by the light it emits, one of two built in or your own measurements, and get its iccMAX profile (section 1);
  • choose an observer (section 2) and make the version 4 profile an ordinary application can use for that observer (section 3);
  • see how far observers agree on the colors of one display, and on two displays matched for one observer (section 4);
  • run your own colors through the profiles and back (section 5).

The profiles are built and used by iccDEV's own tools (iccFromXml, iccV5DspObsToV4Dsp, iccApplyNamedCmm and iccDumpProfile, the International Color Consortium's reference implementation), compiled to WebAssembly and run in this page. They load only here, when first needed. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

Each observer sees a display through its own Wpt normalization, with the display's white as the light. The profile for an observer carries the Wpt MAT from it to the standard PCS (CIE 1931, 2°, under D50), as the profiles of the Observers lab do.

1. The display

Your own display

The light your display emits at full red, full green and full blue (and, if you have it, at black), as measured with a spectroradiometer: a column of wavelengths, then a column for each, in any units. Comma, tab or space separated, header row optional, at least 420 to 680 nm. An uneven grid is brought to every nanometre.

Code values (0 to 1, or 0 to 255, …) and the drive each gives as linear light: one column for all three channels, or one each for red, green and blue; any scale, the last row is full drive. Measure each channel alone and subtract what the display gives at black (the black spectrum above), so that code value 0 has drive 0: the page adds black once, to every color.

The profile (iccMAX version 5, class display) holds the tone curves and the emission of red, green, blue and black every nanometre the display gives, and the way back (the inverse emission and inverse curves). Its own viewing conditions are the CIE 1931 (2°) observer with the display's white as the light; any observer's profile can stand in for them.

2. The observer

The light is always the display's own white. A camera's sensors work too: its "observer profile" is then the camera's.

3. A version 4 profile for this observer

An application that knows only ICC version 4 can still show the display's colors for this observer: iccDEV's iccV5DspObsToV4Dsp takes the display's profile and the observer's, and writes an ordinary matrix and tone curve display profile. Its red, green and blue are what the observer sees of the display's three lights, carried by the observer's Wpt MAT to the standard PCS.

The commands, as on the command line

    
iccDumpProfile of the version 4 profile

    
x and y are the chromaticities of the colorants (their XYZ in the standard PCS). The dashed
    triangle is the CIE 1931 observer's, for comparison. The tone curves are copied from the display's profile,
    sampled at 2048 code values.

5. Run colors through

  

    
Red, green and blue code values, one color per line (0 to 1, or 0 to 255), through iccDEV's color management
    (iccApplyNamedCmm): to CIELAB in the standard PCS (D50), once with the display's profile and the observer's as its
    viewing conditions, once with the version 4 profile of section 3; and back from CIELAB to code values (the round
    trip).

    
    

      
      
    

    

    
The commands, as on the command line

    
ΔE00 is between the two CIELAB values of the same color, from the version 5 profiles
    and from the version 4 one. The round trip goes from the version 5 CIELAB back through the display's profile;
    "back" is the largest difference from the code values put in. A display that gives some light at black (the LCD
    does) parts most from its version 4 profile near black: a matrix and tone curve profile has no place for that
    light, and iccV5DspObsToV4Dsp counts it once in each of red, green and blue, so the version 4 white is a little
    lighter too. A very saturated color can come out of the observer's MAT with X, Y or Z a little below 0 (a narrow
    red's Z, for example); iccDEV clips it on the way back from CIELAB, so that color does not come all the way
    back, and "back" shows by how much.
