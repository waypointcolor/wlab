From the onramp to using material color in practice

The guide, WLab and Wpt: Color Without the Fine Print, is the onramp: it takes you from CIELAB's fine print to the color of a material itself. This page leads from there into the Waypoint Labs, where the same ideas are used on your own data. Each lab works on its own and defines what it uses; this page gathers those ideas in one place, beside the familiar terms they stand next to.

1. Where the onramp leaves you

The guide gave three things. Materials that look alike in one place can be told apart from materials that stay alike everywhere. Every material has a place, Wpt, that stays nearly the same whatever the light and whoever is looking, and WLab reshapes that place so that distances read like familiar ΔE values. And a material adjustment transform (MAT) carries measurements from one light or viewer to another by way of the material.

The labs add the rest of the road: the same ideas tried on your own spectra, sensors and lights, over many lights, observers and objects at once. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you enter or load is sent anywhere.

  • Does WLab hold hue constant as colors get lighter and grayer? Uniformity and hue Lines of constant hue and the Munsell loci in WLab beside CIELAB and CAM16-UCS, and the object-color solid of two conditions.

  • How different is an observer (a person's own, or a camera's) from the standard ones, and how is it carried in an iccMAX profile? Observers The OME and OCE against the CIE observers, the closest CIE 2006 observer, and a profile that carries the observer.
  • How alike do two lights render materials, and how does that sit beside the color rendering index and TM-30? Lighting The IRE over libraries of objects and observers, the lights that come closest, and the CCT and color rendering indices beside it.

3. Ideas the labs share

Observing condition
An observer under a light, shortened to "condition". The observer is any set of sensors: a standard observer's color-matching functions, cone fundamentals (the CIE 2006 model for any age and field size, or curves measured for one person), the categorical observers, or a camera's sensors. A material enters only through its spectral reflectance, so one material can be measured in any number of conditions.
Wpt normalization and its four steps
One 3 × 3 matrix per condition that turns the condition's responses into Wpt, the common representation of materials. It is built in four steps: white is turned onto the first axis, scaled to W = 100, the base colors (40 Munsell colors of Value 5 and Chroma 6, one per hue) are leveled to one W, and their p and t are fitted to the reference's. Wpt has three coordinates: W, for how much light the material reflects (white is 100), and p and t across it (0 for white and every gray).
WLab and ΔEWLab
WLab reshapes Wpt so that a distance in it, ΔEWLab, predicts the color difference the reference observer (CIE 1931 under illuminant C) would see between two materials, whatever condition they were measured in. WLab is not a color appearance model; it is a material color equivalency model: two materials with the same WLab are equivalent as materials.
MAT, and how it differs from a CAT
A material adjustment transform (MAT) is the normalization of one condition followed by the inverse of another's: it predicts what a material measured in the first will measure in the second (material color). A chromatic adaptation transform (CAT: von Kries, Bradford, CAT02) has the same shape but is built from the two lights' white points alone, and it predicts what will look the same to a viewer who has adapted to each light (corresponding color). Both carry white exactly; they differ in what they do to every other color.
Shift and shift manifold
A material's shift is how far and which way its Wpt (or WLab) moves between two conditions. Measured in many conditions, one material makes a small cloud of points, its Wpt shift manifold. How large the cloud is, and its shape, are the material's own behavior.
The equivalency metrics, one family
Each is a mean ΔEWLab over materials, with one thing varied and the rest held, and each is 0 when the thing varied makes no difference to any material.

The libraries, and your own data
Every lab draws on the same libraries: objects (the corrected Munsell colors, the CIE's color evaluation samples, natural objects, skin, soils and others, each with its source and terms), lights (the CIE illuminants, fluorescent and LED lights, Planckian and daylight series) and observers (CIE, CIE 2006, categorical, cameras). Your own spectra, sensors and lights can be pasted or loaded beside them.
