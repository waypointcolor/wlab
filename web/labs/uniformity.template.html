Uniformity and hue

WLab is meant to be a uniform space for material color: equal distances in it should be seen as about equal color differences, and a line of one WLab hue should keep one hue as its colors get lighter and grayer. This page tests that claim. It is being built; its first part, below, is the constant-hue experiment.

Still to come: the loci of constant Munsell hue and chroma in WLab beside CIELAB; constant-hue lines for any observer and light; the object-color solid (the most chromatic colors any material can have) of an observing condition in WLab, and how it changes from one condition to another; and checks against published sets of color-difference and constant-hue data where their licenses allow.

1. Constant hue

Each row is one lightness, from gray (left) to the most vivid color your screen can show. Each chip is split: the lower-right half holds WLab hue constant; the upper-left half has the same CIELAB lightness and chroma but holds CIELAB hue constant, at the hue of the ringed chip. Judge for yourself which half stays closer to the ringed chip's hue as the colors get lighter and grayer.
