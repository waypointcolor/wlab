WLab robustness report

Will a match hold where the product is actually seen? Paste the spectral reflectance of a standard and its samples, choose the lights (and observers) that matter, and read how far each sample is from the standard under each one, as ΔEWLab. Everything runs in this page; nothing is sent anywhere.

1. Spectra

First column wavelength in nm, then one column per material; the first material is the standard. Comma, tab or semicolon separated, with or without a header row. Any range and interval from 380 to 780 nm; reflectance as 0–1 or in percent.

    3. Report

    ΔEWLab predicts the color difference the reference observer (CIE 1931, under illuminant C) would see between two materials, whatever light and observer they were measured under, in units that behave like familiar ΔE units. No tolerances are built in: as with ΔE00, experience with your own products should set the limits. Chips are approximate sRGB renderings under D65.

    Method: WLab and Wpt: Color Without the Fine Print (M. W. Derhak) and the Wpt and WLab papers (Derhak and Berns, Color Research & Application 2015). Built on the wlab package. CIE data CC BY-SA 4.0.
