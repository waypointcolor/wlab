WLab and Wpt

Color without the fine print: a material's color for every light and every viewer

Every CIELAB value comes with fine print: it holds for one light and one standard viewer. Wpt (pronounced "Waypoint") describes the material instead, and WLab (Waypoint Lab) reshapes Wpt so that distances read like familiar ΔE color differences. Try the two demos below, then read the guide.

The short version

  • A CIELAB value describes a sample under one light, as one standard viewer sees it. Change the light or the viewer and the value changes, by a different amount for each sample.
  • That is why two samples that match in the viewing booth (a cabinet lit by one standard light, where colors are checked) can stop matching in the store. The name for it is metamerism.
  • Wpt and WLab are coordinates for the material itself: for typical materials they stay nearly the same whatever the light and whoever is looking, and how far a material does move is useful information in itself. WLab differences are designed to be similar in size to familiar ΔE values.
  • With them you can ask questions that a check under the booth's one light cannot answer. Will this match hold under store lighting? Does this ink hold its color? Do these two viewers, or a camera and a person, agree?
  • A material adjustment transform (MAT) carries measurements from one light and/or viewer to another by way of the material.
  • Keep CIELAB and ΔE00 for work under one light and for contracts. Reach for WLab when a comparison crosses lights, viewers or devices.

For developers

Everything on this page runs on wlab, a small TypeScript implementation of Wpt, WLab and MATs with no dependencies, tested against the reference implementation. It takes spectra for any light and observer, and display colors in sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB or Rec. 2020.

import { wptNormalization, deltaEWlab, rgbToWlab } from 'wlab';

const store = wptNormalization('cie1931', 'F11');      // one normalization per light and observer
const d = deltaEWlab(store.wlabOf(standard), store.wlabOf(sample));   // spectra: 380–780 nm, 5 nm

rgbToWlab([0.2, 0.6, 0.3], 'display-p3');              // a display color, through D65

A display color is not a material: in WLab it stands for the material that would give the same numbers under D65. The guide's Technical annex (A.8) maps every equation to its function.

ΔEWLab predicts the color difference the reference observer (CIE 1931, under illuminant C) would see between two materials, whatever light and observer they were measured under. WLab is not a color appearance model.
